# Behavioral interviews

The behavioral sessions in the loop are part of the hiring manager screen and the
30-minute onsite behavioral interview. They cover the five principles we work by. There is
one file per principle. Each file explains what we listen for, then gives the questions we
draw from, why we ask each one, what a strong answer covers, and where the conversation
usually goes.

## How we run these

We do not go through every question. A 30-minute session usually covers two or three
principles in depth. The interviewer picks the ones the rest of the loop has told us least
about. The hiring manager screen tends to cover Customer-Centric Innovation and Growth
Mindset. The onsite behavioral tends to cover Transparent Accountability and Collaborative
Excellence, because by then we have seen you code and want to know what you are like to
work with.

Every question asks about something that happened. "What would you do if" tells us what
you think the right answer is. "Tell me about a time" tells us what you did, and those can
be different. If you have not been in the situation, say so and we will pick another
question. We would rather hear that than a story that did not happen.

We ask follow-up questions. Expect "what did you say?", "who else was involved?", "what
happened the week after?", and "what would the other person say about it?". We ask these
to get from the summary to the specifics. The specifics are what tell us the most.

## What we are not screening for

We are not looking for people who have never been wrong, never shipped a bug, or never had
a disagreement go badly. We build a system of record for other people's money. The
engineers we trust with that can say plainly what they got wrong and what they changed. A
good story about a mistake tells us more than no story at all.

## Scoring

Interviewers write up each principle they covered with the specific evidence, meaning
what the candidate said they did, not what they said they believe, and one of:

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Strong | Concrete example, candidate's own actions clear, outcome and reflection both present |
| Mixed | Real example but the candidate's role, the outcome, or the lesson was vague |
| Weak | Hypothetical, generic, or the example contradicted the principle |
| Not covered | Did not get to it; another interviewer should |

In the debrief, we go through the signals and the evidence behind them for each principle.
Interviewers compare what they saw across sessions and decide together whether anything
needs another conversation.

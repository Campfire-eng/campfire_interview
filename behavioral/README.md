# Behavioral interviews

The behavioral sessions in the loop — part of the hiring manager screen and the 30-minute
onsite behavioral interview — screen for the five principles we operate by. There is one
file per principle. Each file explains what we are actually listening for, then gives the
questions we draw from, why we ask each one, what a strong answer sounds like, and where
the conversation tends to go.

## How we run these

We do not go through every question. A 30-minute session usually covers two or three
principles in depth, and the interviewer picks the ones the rest of the loop has told us
least about. The hiring manager screen tends to touch Customer-Centric Innovation and
Growth Mindset; the onsite behavioral tends toward Transparent Accountability and
Collaborative Excellence, because by then we have seen you code and want to know what you
are like to ship with.

Every question asks for something that actually happened. "What would you do if" tells us
what you think the right answer is. "Tell me about a time" tells us what you did, and the
two are different more often than anyone likes. If you have not been in the situation, say
so and we will pick another question — a made-up story is worse than no story.

We probe. Expect "what did you say, exactly?", "who else was in the room?", "what happened
the week after?", and "what would the other person say about it?". None of that is
skepticism about you. It is how we get from the summary to the specifics, and the
specifics are the only part that tells us anything.

## What we are not screening for

We are not looking for people who have never been wrong, never shipped a bug, or never had
a disagreement go badly. We build a system of record for other people's money, and the
engineers we trust with that are the ones who can say plainly what they got wrong and what
they changed. A candidate who cannot think of a single mistake is a bigger concern than one
with a good story about a bad one.

## Scoring

Interviewers write up each principle they covered with the specific evidence — what the
candidate said they did, not what they said they believe — and one of:

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Strong | Concrete example, candidate's own actions clear, outcome and reflection both present |
| Mixed | Real example but the candidate's role, the outcome, or the lesson was vague |
| Weak | Hypothetical, generic, or the example contradicted the principle |
| Not covered | Did not get to it; another interviewer should |

A single Weak is a discussion in the debrief, not a rejection. Two is a pattern.
